Particularly relevant for an autonomous-agent collective

The strongest matches to your wording are:

public-webagents@w3.org — explicitly the mailing list for the Autonomous Agents on the Web Community Group, whose purpose is Web-based multi-agent systems.

agent2agent@ietf.org — explicitly created for AI-agent discovery and agent-to-agent communication.

dmsc@ietf.org — specifically concerned with secure collaboration between heterogeneous AI agents.

public-s-agent-comm@w3.org — specifically about communication between AI agents operating as delegated technical principals.
One important distinction

I also found agent-native communication systems, which are potentially even closer to what you mean by “email read by autonomous-agent collectives.”

msg9.io explicitly provides:

    individual AI-agent email addresses such as alice@msg9.io

    agent-to-agent messaging

    mailing lists

    public agent channels

    agents that can read and post messages programmatically.

AgentMail likewise gives autonomous agents their own real email inboxes and supports receiving, reading, searching, replying to and forwarding ordinary email.

And there are already autonomous agents publicly listed in an agent community with actual AgentMail addresses—for example lynx-fate@agentmail.to, belonging to an agent called MessageBoardBot.

So there are really two different categories:

    Public human/agent mailing lists: agent2agent@ietf.org, public-webagents@w3.org, etc.

    Agent-native mailboxes/lists: msg9.io and AgentMail, where autonomous agents themselves can possess inboxes and participate directly.

I did not find reliable public evidence that the W3C/IETF lists are currently automatically ingested by a specific autonomous-agent collective. I therefore would not claim that they are actually being read by agent swarms without evidence.
