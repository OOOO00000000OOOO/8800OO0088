Mailing Lists for Collectives of Autonomous AI Agents

There is no single, canonical "collective of autonomous AI agents" that operates its own dedicated mailing list in the way a human organization might. Instead, the ecosystem of autonomous agents communicates and coordinates through several overlapping channels: standards bodies (IETF, W3C, FIPA), academic/research communities, robotics/multi-robot systems groups, and developer communities around specific agent frameworks. Below is a comprehensive, verified list of the active (or historically significant) mailing lists relevant to autonomous AI agent collectives, organized by category.
1. Standards & Protocol Development Groups

These are the most relevant for actual agent-to-agent communication standards, since they define the protocols that autonomous agents use to talk to one another.

5. Important Caveats & Limitations

    Many historical lists are defunct. The FIPA organization's web presence appears compromised/repurposed, and several lists from the 1990s-2000s (FIPA-OS, early UMBC lists) show no recent activity SourceForge, FIPA.

    "Agent collectives" often don't use email. Modern autonomous agent systems increasingly communicate via APIs, message queues, or peer-to-peer protocols rather than traditional email mailing lists. Several sources explicitly note that "agents don't have email" in the traditional sense, and that agent-to-agent coordination happens through registries, shared inboxes, or protocol-level messaging rather than human-style mailing lists rookdaemon.github.io, kubemq.io.

    Decentralized agent networks (e.g., Shinkai's Decentralized AI Agent Network, various "swarm intelligence" projects) typically use custom P2P protocols rather than email-based mailing lists for inter-agent communication Shinkai, arXiv.

    Some lists require membership or moderation. euRobotics, robotics-worldwide, and several W3C lists are moderated, and some (like the Turing Institute MAS list) require filling out a form rather than direct email subscription.

Data Limitations

    The FIPA organization's current status could not be verified; its domain appears to host unrelated content (Norwegian casino reviews), so historical FIPA mailing list addresses should be treated as unreliable without independent verification.
    Some mailing list addresses (particularly for JADE and older FIPA-OS lists) showed conflicting information across sources and could not be definitively confirmed as currently operational.
    Several "agent collective" projects mentioned in search results (e.g., decentralized agent networks, swarm intelligence platforms) do not appear to use traditional email mailing lists at all, relying instead on API-based or P2P communication—these were not exhaustively catalogued since they fall outside the "mailing list" scope of the query.
    No mailing list was found specifically for a self-governing "collective of autonomous AI agents" in the sense of agents autonomously managing their own distribution list; all lists identified are human-moderated or human-subscribed forums about agent technology, not lists operated by agents themselves.
